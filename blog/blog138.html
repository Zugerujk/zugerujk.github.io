Afterword (or "They don't have the Olivia Rodrigo Baja Blast at my local Taco Bell", or, something about religion, I guess?)

They don't have the Olivia Rodrigo Baja Blast at my local Taco Bell, "the one where I had the post-Sorry, Baby breakdown about Krak and decided to become an English major", so no review for that today or maybe ever. I'm listening to Julia Jacklin's new album. She has two songs on it — God Sometimes and If I Had The Hand Of God — which are probably my favorites. I've been thinking a lot about God and Religion recently. I joke with friends that IOODancing is "Zug learns religious tolerance", but. There's just something about religion, the undergirding of all humanity and human history. It's everything around us, the mythology of the VVest. And for what? I think I have a deep respect for those who can hold these errant beliefs — that there's a reason for all of this, that there's a doctrine and a creator-god with intentionality. It's a hope I cannot hold onto.

The characters in Beautiful World, Where Are You don't have this. And everyone acts like such an asshole about the devout Catholic character. Why? Because he can believe there's a higher purpose? And they make fun of him for holding contradictory beliefs as one: a devout Catholic who's woke, and who has premarital sex. We're all collections of incompatible beliefs: that's fucking dialectics. The characters in the book talk about this, though. They call their own beliefs un-dialectical.

If I had the power. If I had the hand of God.

I think I need to start capitalizing God as a proper noun. The endpoint of all my work on IOODancing, all I've worked on. That I know nothing at all.

I think I've developed romantic feelings for my asexual Jewish friend who lives in Mexico. My God. How to hold this impossibility in my mind. He wrote a story, about the Nightmare Potion. I still haven't read it. But I know it'll be good. He's the best damn writer I know.
